Lab 14 · threat intelligence · the finale

One indicator, the whole house.

A single confirmed bad indicator is a loose thread. Pull it through the things the adversary reused — a TLS certificate, a registration pattern, a favicon, a passive-DNS history — and the rest of their infrastructure comes with it. This is the analyst's multiplier: one domain becomes a map. The craft is knowing which threads are the adversary's own and which are shared by half the internet, because the fastest way to burn a day is to pivot onto a CDN and “discover” Cloudflare.

Passive & analysis only. Every pivot here is open-source enrichment of an indicator you already have — passive DNS, certificate transparency, WHOIS, public scan data. Nothing scans, probes, or touches the adversary's live infrastructure.
01 — why it works

Attackers reuse, and reuse leaves links

Infrastructure costs effort, so operators recycle it: the same self-signed cert across panels, the same registrant identity, the same host, the same hand-built C2 landing page (and thus the same favicon). Each reused attribute is an edge you can traverse from a known-bad node to its unknown siblings. The whole game is edge quality: pivot on something specific to this adversary, not something they happen to share with everyone.

strong edgesAttributes rare enough to be near-unique: a specific self-signed TLS cert (SHA-1/SHA-256), a reused registrant email, a hand-rolled panel's favicon hash (mmh3), a distinctive JARM/JA3S TLS fingerprint. A match here is a real link.
weak edgesAttributes shared by millions: a cloud ASN, a CDN IP, a privacy-proxy WHOIS, a Let's Encrypt issuer. A match here means almost nothing on its own — it's a hint, not a link.
the methodExpand on strong edges, corroborate across two+ independent edges before you claim a node, and prune anything that only connects through a weak edge. Confidence is per-edge, and the cluster is only as good as its weakest included link.

03 — the pivot set

What each thread reveals — and its trap

passive DNSHistorical domain↔IP resolutions from recursive-resolver telemetry (Farsight/DNSDB-style). Reveals every domain that ever pointed here. Trap: shared/parked hosting means co-residents who aren't related.
TLS certificateCertificate Transparency + scan data: same cert hash, SANs, or self-signed subject across hosts. Strong when the cert is bespoke. Trap: wildcard/shared-panel certs and Let's Encrypt reuse.
WHOIS / registrantRegistrant email, org, nameservers, registration timing. Reused identity or same-day bulk registration is a strong cluster. Trap: privacy proxies redact nearly everything now.
favicon hashmmh3 hash of the site's favicon (Shodan/Censys indexable). A hand-built C2 panel reused across hosts shares an exact favicon. Trap: default framework favicons are everywhere.
JARM / JA3SActive/observed TLS-stack fingerprints. Clusters hosts running the same C2 server software. Trap: popular stacks (nginx, Cobalt-Strike-lookalikes) collide with legit servers.
ASN / netblockThe hosting network. Useful as context (bulletproof hosters recur). Trap: never a link by itself — an ASN holds millions of unrelated hosts.
04 — the hard part

The fastest way to be wrong

  • Shared infrastructure is a bystander factory. Pivot onto a Cloudflare IP or a shared host and you'll “find” thousands of unrelated sites. Every one you include is a false accusation. Weak edges get pruned, not clustered.
  • WHOIS is mostly dark now. Post-GDPR redaction and privacy proxies mean registrant pivots often dead-end. Absence of a link isn't absence of a relationship.
  • Passive beats active — and it's the legal line. Enriching an indicator from OSINT and passive telemetry is analysis. Actively scanning or connecting to the adversary's infrastructure is a different activity with different rules; this lab stays on the passive side of that line, and so should you.
  • A cluster is a hypothesis. Two independent strong edges make a confident link; one makes a lead. Everything you assemble feeds back into the timeline (Lab 7) and the actor model (Lab 13) as evidence, not verdict.
